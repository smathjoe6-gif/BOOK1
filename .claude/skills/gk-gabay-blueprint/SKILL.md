## GK Classical Poetry Library (Joe's own collection, added 21 Oct 2026)

Joe gave this set directly as GK Legend Studio's own poetry library (his
material, not a third-party reference) — stored here in full for the
brand to draw on and for Claude to learn the ruling-letter pattern from.
Five Gabay + five Buraanbur, each locked to one ruling sound
(xaraf-raac), as the "How a gabay works" rule above requires:

**Part 1 — Five Classical Gabay**

1. *Xididka iyo Hiddaha* (The Root and the Heritage) — vowel-locked:
   "Nin rag ahu halkuu udub u xidho, waa ogyahay halkee / Arlaadaan
   carraheed ku yaal, waana loo oggolyahaye / Alindiga la tolayoiyo
   xariir, waa asaaskii e / Anagoo ah ehelkii sharaftoo, aannaan
   oggolaaneyn / Inaan meel marinno taariikhda, awowyaasheen
   abaahayood."
2. *Kaban iyo Kheyraad* (The Kaban and the Legacy) — K-locked: "Kaban
   yahay adduunyadaada iyo, kiishka maansada e / Kolba tii la jaanqaadda
   guud, waa kow iyo taami e / Kor u qaad halkii ay ka timid,
   guutooyinkii hore e / Kibir iyo aqoon aan lahayn, kuma xirnoo garab
   e / Nin kastuu ka soo jeedo asalkaa, lagu xisaabtamaaye."
3. *Geesiga Koonfureed* (The Southern Hero) — Q-locked: "Koonfurtaan
   qorraxdu ay ka soo baxdo, qadarin bay leede / Qabiil iyo qoys bay ka
   dhalatay, qaran la yaabane / Qarankaynu leennahayna waa, qori iyo
   qawl adag e / Qofkii doonaow maanka geli, qaddarinta ay leede /
   Qoyskiinna GK waa astaan, qarniyo raagaysaaye."
4. *Waaq iyo Waayaha Cusub* (The Era and the New Dawn) — W-locked:
   "Waaqii abuurayay adduunyada, wuu inooq qoray e / Waddooyinka la
   marayaa waa, weji ku caan ah e / Waayaha adduunyada maanta iyo,
   wacdiga maansada e / Warkaygu waa mid waafaqsan oo, wacan ku soo
   socda e / Waaya-arag rag baa kicitimoo, waxtar u keenay e."
5. *Modern Legend iyo Maankayaga* (Modern Legend & Our Mind) — M-locked:
   "Maankaygu waa kii bartoo, maamus ku abqaalaye / Modern Legend-ka aan
   taagannahay, waa mid soo jire e / Meelaha la joogaba astaan,
   lagama maarmaan ah e / Magacaaga GK baa ku xardhan, muunki iyo
   seerke e / Marnaba lagama guurotooo, waa mid sugan dhab ahe."

**Part 2 — Five Traditional Buraanbur**

1. *Taarka Kaban iyo Hodanka* (The Strands of Kaban and Wealth) —
   H-locked: "Hallow baa la yiri maansadii, halyeeygii dhashaye / Hodan
   baannu nahay reer GK, hiddaha keenaye / Haweenkii xidhmada
   xirtayoo, harqaan lagu tumaye / Hibo iyo aqoon bay wataan, hooyadiis
   dhalaye!"
2. *Alindi iyo Sharaf* (Alindi and Honor) — vowel-locked: "Arlaadii
   nabaddaydii baa, lagu ogaadeene / Alindiga la joogiyo xariir, waa
   mid oran jiife / Inaynu isku duubnawnayoo, ehel la leennahay e /
   Ubadkiinna soo kacaya iyo, ab Abtirsiinyow e!"
3. *Geesiga Guulaystay* (The Victorious Hero) — G-locked: "Guul baa
   dhalata oo maanta, geesi soo kace e / Guryahaana loo soo degey,
   gacan ku rimis ahe / Guntiinada la xirtayoo, garabka loo saare / G
   iyo K waa calaamaddii, guudka laga sooce!"
4. *Dhaanto iyo Ciyaarta* (Dhaanto and the Dance) — Dh-locked:
   "Dhaantadii la tumayayoo, dheddig iyo labe e / Dhexdaadaada loo
   jeediyoo, dhab u haysanaye / Dhedo iyo abaartaannu marin,
   dhalashadiis ahe / Dhulka hoostiisii baannu jirro, dhawri
   doonnaaye!"
5. *Himilo iyo Himey* (Ambition and Legacy) — H-locked: "Heesaha la
   tiriyiyo habkii, haadka loo kacaye / Himiladiinnii baannu helay,
   hoos u eegid ahe / Haybaddii sharaftaydii baa, hagaysa reerkeenna /
   Hadda iyo weliba dambena, waa hodan adduunke!"

This is GK's own material (brand references itself: "reer GK", "Modern
Legend-ka", "Magacaaga GK", "G iyo K"), so unlike a third-party reference
track it's fine to draw actual lines from it, not just the pattern —
still check with Joe before quoting a line publicly, same as any other
verse source.

## Iron rules

1. **Joe writes the Somali. Claude never invents Somali verse.** Keep Joe's
   lines exactly as sent — no "fixes". If a word looks like a typo (e.g.
   "ururkaoo", "Atkutaba"), leave it and ask in one line.
2. **Somali words Claude may use on its own:** only real, well-known ones
   (see vocabulary below) or ones fact-checked from a named source. **Why
   this is an iron rule, not a style preference** (Joe, 19 Oct 2026 — source:
   "Autopsy of an Algorithmic Hallucination," a forensic breakdown of a real
   YouTube auto-caption run on a Somali track): when an AI system has no real
   data for a low-resource language like Somali, it doesn't fail cleanly —
   it pattern-matches the input to the nearest *high-resource* language it
   does have deep data for (in that case, Arabic script) and confidently
   outputs fluent-looking nonsense (the deck's example: real Somali vocals
   came out as a looping "راعي راعي راعي" — invented ghost text, not a real
   transcription). This is the exact same failure mode as an LLM inventing
   fake-sounding "Somali" phrasing instead of real words — confident-looking
   output standing in for data that was never there. It's why this skill
   insists on only real, checked vocabulary: the fix isn't "try harder," it's
   "never let the model freewheel in a language it doesn't actually have."
3. **Nothing is generated (audio/image/video) until Joe says "Go" /
   "Generate now".** Before that it's text only.

## How a gabay works (check Joe's verse against this)

- **Qaafiyad (alliteration):** ONE sound runs through the WHOLE poem.
- A gabay line has two halves; **each half needs at least one word** starting
  with that sound. Not every word — one per half-line.
- **All vowels count as the same sound** (A, E, I, O, U alliterate together).
- Gabay = long (14–16 syllables per line, traditionally 100+ lines), formal,
  weighty. Geeraar = short, urgent, punchy. Buraanbur = women's form,
  drum + call-and-answer. A song uses a gabay *excerpt*, so say "gabay-style"
  when it's short.
- When a letter is chosen, list which word carries it in each half-line and
  flag any half-line that's missing it (don't rewrite — ask Joe).

## Checked facts (safe for captions)

- Gabay: 14–16 syllables per line, often 100+ lines, the prestige form; one
  alliteration sound through the whole poem, in each half-line.
- Poems were memorised and carried by reciters (hafidayaal); the alliteration
  helps memory. Say "easy to memorise and pass on" — NOT "coded messages".
- Sayid Maxamed Cabdulle Xasan used gabay as resistance against colonial rule.
- Buraanbur: women's poetry, woman poet with drum in the circle, call-and-answer,
  one dancer per family (shawl over face, one-leg step), ululation; used in the
  1940s–50s to rally resistance to colonial rule. Hawa Jibril (1920–2011),
  Mudug plateau, first poem at 12.
- **Golden Age state-sponsored "supergroups" (Joe, 19 Oct 2026):** after
  Maxamed Siyaad Barre's 1969 coup, the government took direct control of
  the arts — commandeering the National Theatre in Mogadishu (opened 1967,
  a gift from China's Chairman Mao Zedong) and attaching major bands
  (Waabeeri, Shareero, Iftin, Horseed, Somaali Jaaz, Onkod, Halgan) to the
  government, military or police, unlike independent groups like the
  Dur-Dur Band — their repertoire was heavily patriotic/propagandised. This
  era drove a shift from unaccompanied poetry to musical theatre ("muusiko"),
  and trained key figures: Maryan Mursal ("first lady of Somali jazz,"
  Waabeeri → Somaali Jaaz), Abdullahi Qarshe, Ali Feiruz, Magool, Hassan
  Sheikh Mumin, Abdi Ali ("Bacalwaan"). The bands also had nightclub/hotel
  residencies in "swinging Mogadishu" (Somaali Jaaz at the Jubba and
  Al-Uruba hotels, Iftin at the Lido nightclub, Shareero at the Jazira
  hotel) alongside the state theatre circuit. Useful background for
  Documentary-lane/history content — don't reproduce any band's actual
  song lyrics in a caption or doc; summarize the history, don't quote the
  art.
- **Theater culture as a standing content theme (Joe, 20 Oct 2026):** this
  National Theatre/musical-theatre history above (the 1967 Mogadishu
  National Theatre, the shift to "muusiko," the state theatre circuit,
  nightclub residencies in "swinging Mogadishu") is now a reach-for-it-by-
  name theme — use it whenever Joe asks for something in a "theater
  culture" direction: documentary-lane Drive docs on the real history, or
  a song/video concept that leans into theatrical staging energy (the
  stage, the spotlight, the performance tradition) grounded in these real,
  checked facts — never invented theater history, and never reproducing
  any band's actual lyrics.
- **Balwo origin (Joe, 19 Oct 2026):** founded by Abdi Sinimo, a Somali
  lorry driver from the Reer Nuur subclan of the Gadabuursi, born out of a
  spontaneous moment of frustration (c. 1943–1945) when his truck broke
  down in the bush near Zeila — he sang a short poem of distress ("Belwooy,
  belwooy…"), named the new style balwo ("misfortune"), and it became an
  instant public hit that spread to other poets and directly led to the
  more complex heello genre. Simple, personal lyricism about love, passion
  and suffering — the first modern break from classical gabay formality.
- **Why real Somali vocabulary is a hard rule, not a style choice (Joe,
  19 Oct 2026):** the real song "Dardaaran" by Mohamed BK and Nimco Yaasiin
  (2015) is the actual case study behind a forensic write-up Joe shared
  ("Autopsy of an Algorithmic Hallucination") showing a YouTube auto-caption
  system transcribing real Somali vocals as looping, invented Arabic-script
  nonsense ("راعي راعي راعي…") because it had no real Somali data and
  pattern-matched to the nearest high-resource language instead of failing
  cleanly. Same failure mode as an LLM inventing fake-sounding "Somali"
  lines — confident output standing in for data that isn't there. This is
  the grounding for the standing rule above: only real, checked vocabulary,
  never invented-but-plausible-sounding Somali.
- Flag: sky-blue with a white five-pointed star. Independence/union 1 July 1960.
- "Soomaaliyeey toosoo" (early 1940s); "Qolobaa Calankeed" (music Abdullahi
  Qarshe; national anthem since 2012). Somali Youth League founded 1943 (not
  1947, not "by women").
- **Abdullahi Qarshe, "Father of Somali music" (Joe, 19 Oct 2026):** the
  pivotal figure who introduced the kaban (oud) as instrumental
  accompaniment to Somali poetry — before him, early genres like balwo were
  largely vocal-only. Giving the kaban a musical structure to Somali metrical
  poetry directly drove balwo's evolution into the more complex heello
  genre, and Qarshe is recognized as one of heello's first major
  interpreters. He went on to found the modern Qaraami genre by being the
  first to set Qaraami poetry to instrumental music. While working for the
  British colonial administration and asked to compose a welcome song for a
  new governor in Berbera, he exploited the officials' unfamiliarity with
  Somali and instead wrote "Ka kacay!" ("Arise!") — a covert anti-colonial
  rallying cry hidden inside what the British thought was a welcome song.
  Later a leading figure at the National Theatre in Mogadishu.
- **Somali alliteration — the precise rule (Joe, 19 Oct 2026):** applies to
  almost all Somali verse, classical maanso and hees (song) alike. Every
  line or half-line (genre-dependent) needs a word starting with the poem's
  designated sound, AND that word must carry real lexical weight — a noun,
  verb, adjective or adverb, never a minor grammatical particle used just to
  "cheat" the rule. Combined with Somali's quantitative metrical system
  (patterned short/long vowels), this is what gives the verse form its
  tight, memorable structure. Rare exceptions exist (a poem changing or
  briefly dropping its sound) but are uncommon — don't treat them as
  license to loosen the discipline in a GK song.
- **Buraanbur as political speech, not just celebration (Joe, 19 Oct
  2026):** buraanbur has a real history of challenging power, not only
  marking weddings/pride. 1962: when Faduma Alim became the first Somali
  woman to earn a university degree but was then denied a seat in
  parliament, her mother chanted a buraanbur voicing collective anguish at
  how women's sacrifices were being erased by the men "who divided and
  deceived our nation" — asking "Sisters; was this what we struggled for?"
  Decades later, poet Adar Abdi Fiidow composed buraanbur explicitly urging
  Somalis to reject clannism, disarm, and resolve the civil war through
  dialogue rather than the sword, including lines encouraging wives to
  preach peace at home. Modern jazz singer Maryan Mursal (Waabeeri →
  Somaali Jaaz) broke barriers as the first woman to sing Somali jazz and
  to drive a taxi, deliberately using both to show women could be anything
  they chose. More recently, Somali women composed a buraanbur in solidarity
  with the Black Lives Matter movement after George Floyd's murder. Useful
  grounding for any buraanbur content that wants real weight behind it —
  don't invent a "protest buraanbur" moment; these are the real, checked
  ones to draw on or reference.
- **Regional context, Horn of Africa music as resistance (background only,
  not GK's own repertoire):** the same pattern — music smuggling political
  messages past censors — shows up region-wide. Eritrea: Tewolde Redda's
  "Shigey Habuni" ("Give me my torch") was an anthem against forced
  federation with Ethiopia; Ateweberhan Segid's "Aslamai Kistanay" warned
  Christians and Muslims alike against colonial "divide and conquer"
  tactics. Sudan: Kamal Keila sang social-justice tracks like "African
  Unity"; Mohammed Wardi was imprisoned over a song criticizing CIA
  complicity in Patrice Lumumba's 1960 assassination. Not Somali material,
  so don't use it as a Somali fact — useful only as comparative context if
  a documentary-lane post ever widens to the broader Horn of Africa.
