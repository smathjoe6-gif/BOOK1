# Walsh Digital Nets

A digital net's algebra is **addition of digits in base `b`** (almost always `b=2`), not addition modulo 1. Sobol' / Joe-Kuo constructions (`references/joe-kuo.md`) are the base-2 case. Rank-1 lattices `{sz/N}` (`references/lattice-rff.md`) run on the other algebra entirely. **Mixing the two figures of merit is a category error** — the `P2` figure used for lattice CBC (`scripts/cbc_lattice.py`, `scripts/cbc_fft.py`) is a **Fourier** figure; what digital nets optimize is a **Walsh** figure. Same job — score a point set for integration on the cube — different harmonic analysis, and the numbers are not interchangeable.

## Two harmonic languages

An integrand on `[0,1)^p` can be expanded in:

- **Fourier** characters `e^(2πi h·x)`, `h ∈ ℤ^p` — natural for integration lattices `{sz/N}`.
- **Walsh** functions `wal_k(x)`, `k ∈ ℕ0^p` — natural for digital nets whose points are generated by matrix multiplies over a finite field.

Full treatment of the Walsh functions themselves (Paley order, the group-character algebra, orthogonality, the Rademacher/Haar relationship, fast evaluation): `references/walsh-functions.md`. Short version here — a one-dimensional Walsh function, in base `b`: write `x = Σ ξi b⁻ⁱ` and `k = Σ κi bⁱ`. Then:

```
wal_k(x) = exp( (2πi/b) Σ κi ξ(i+1) )
```

For `b=2` this takes values `±1`: `wal_k(x) = (-1)^(Σ κi ξ(i+1))`. In `p` dimensions, `wal_k(x) = Πj wal_{kj}(xj)`. **These are the characters of the dyadic group, not of the circle.** A digital net is built so that many low-digit `wal_k` average to exactly 0 on the point set — the Walsh analogue of "the lattice annihilates low Fourier modes."

If a Sobol' net gets scored with `P2 = Σ |f̂(h)|² / ‖h‖²` (the lattice figure), that's asking a Fourier question of a dyadic object. The number will move around, but it will not be the quantity the construction was actually designed to shrink.

## Generating matrices

A digital net with `N = b^m` points in `p` dimensions is specified by `p` matrices `C1, ..., Cp ∈ 𝔽_b^(m×m)` (or `∞×m` for a sequence).

Index `i = 0, ..., b^m - 1` as a digit column `ī ∈ 𝔽_b^m`. Coordinate `j` is:

```
x⃗(i,j) = Cj · ī ∈ 𝔽_b^m,     x(i,j) = Σ(r=1..m) x⃗(i,j)_r · b⁻ʳ
```

Sobol' is this picture with `b=2` and a very specific family of `Cj`: column `k` of `Cj` is the bits of the direction vector `v(k,j) = m(k,j)·2⁻ᵏ` — the free odd `m(1:sj, j)` plus Sobol's recurrence fill the rest of the matrix. Dimension 1 is the identity (plain van der Corput). Joe-Kuo (`references/joe-kuo.md`) only choose those free bits and which primitive polynomial labels dimension `j`.

A **polynomial lattice** is the same digital picture with `Cj` coming from a modulus polynomial and generating polynomials — Walsh CBC lives more cleanly here than on free Sobol' seeds, since the construction is explicitly optimized against a Walsh figure from the start.

## Dual net and `t`-value

The **Walsh dual** is the set of multi-indices `k` such that `wal_k` equals 1 on every point — the modes the net does *not* kill. Quality means "the dual avoids cheap `k`."

Niederreiter's `t` for a `(t,m,p)`-net in base `b`: every elementary interval `Πj [aj·b^(-dj), (aj+1)·b^(-dj)]` with `Σ dj = m-t` contains exactly `b^t` points. Smaller `t` is better; `t=0` is a perfect dyadic stratification in every aligned box of volume `b^(-m)`.

A 2-D projection of a Sobol' sequence can have a large `t` even when every 1-D projection is perfect (Property A) — that's the stripe pattern Joe-Kuo were fixing: they search the free `m(k,j)` so those 2-D `t`-values stay small (`references/joe-kuo.md`).

## The Walsh `P_α` figure

For a digital net with `N = b^m` points, the unweighted Walsh figure (even `α`, base `b`) looks like:

```
P_α^Wal = Σ_{k ∈ D \ {0}} 1 / r_α(k)
```

where `D` is the **dual net** in the Walsh sense (the digit-generating matrices' orthogonal complement), and `r_α(k)` is a product over coordinates of a digit-wise weight, typically:

```
r_α(kj) = 1              if kj = 0
        = b^(α·⌊log_b kj⌋)   if kj > 0
```

(up to the exact Dick-Pillichshammer / Nuyens normalization). Small `P_α^Wal` means the dual has no cheap, low-digit `k` — the net kills the Walsh modes the construction actually cares about.

Product weights `γj` enter exactly as they do in the lattice `P2`: coordinate `j` gets scaled by `γj^α` so later or less important axes are allowed to be worse.

There is again a **product-form identity** over the points, analogous to lattice `P2`'s `-1 + (1/N) Σs Πj (1 + γj·2π²·B2({xs,j}))` — but the 1-D kernel here is a Walsh kernel (a piecewise-constant, digitally defined `ωα(x)`), not `B2`. Fast CBC exists in this world too: the search over generating matrices or polynomial-lattice coefficients becomes an FFT **over the finite field / over polynomials**, not over `ℤ/Nℤ` the way `scripts/cbc_fft.py` works.

`P_α^Wal` is a **weighted sum over the dual**, not a single `t`-value — it's the figure fast digital CBC actually minimizes. Joe-Kuo did not publish a Walsh-`P_α` CBC of Sobol' seeds; what they published is a 2-D `t` search plus Property A, a related but distinct optimization target.

## Scrambles (still Walsh)

An **Owen scramble** applies random permutations to digit trees. A **digital shift** XORs a random digit vector onto each coordinate. Both preserve the net's `t` (in expectation, or exactly for a digital shift) and give `R` independent copies for a crude RMSE estimate. **Neither turns a Sobol' net into a lattice.**

Gray-code generation is just a fast way to walk `i ↦ i ⊕ (i>>1)` so each new point costs one XOR of a direction vector — same point set as the matrix definition, just a different traversal order.

## Construction checklist (base 2)

1. Pick primitive polynomials, one per coordinate `j ≥ 2`.
2. Pick free odd `m(1:sj, j) < 2^k` (a Joe-Kuo table, or a Walsh-CBC of a polynomial lattice).
3. Recur the remaining `m(k,j)`; form the bits of `Cj`.
4. Emit `x(i,j)` by XOR of direction vectors, or equivalently `Cj·ī`.
5. Optional: Owen scramble / digital shift.
6. For RFF: map `xi` through `F_Λ⁻¹`. **That step leaves Walsh-land entirely** — see `references/qmc-rff.md`.

## What the algebra does not give you

- `{sz/N}` from a Joe-Kuo `m(k,j)` — there is no such mapping.
- A Fourier `P2` that means the same thing as a Walsh `P_α` — they score different harmonic content.
- Exact interpolation of `cos(ωᵀδ)` after the inverse-cdf — that integrand is not a low-digit Walsh polynomial any more than it's a low-degree trigonometric polynomial.

The construction details end at producing a good point set on the cube. Bochner error (`references/rff.md`) only starts after `F_Λ⁻¹` is applied — neither the Fourier nor the Walsh figure is that true RFF error; both are cheap proxies for "this point set is not stupid on the cube."

## Versus Fourier lattice CBC

| Point set | Figure that matches the algebra | Use for RFF `ω` |
|---|---|---|
| Rank-1 lattice `{sz/N}` | Fourier `P_α`, `B_α` | Yes (`scripts/cbc_lattice.py` / `scripts/cbc_fft.py`) |
| Sobol' / digital net | Walsh `P_α`, `t`-value | Yes (published nets + scramble, `references/joe-kuo.md`) |
| Polynomial lattice | Walsh `P_α` over `𝔽_b[x]` | Yes, if built that way |
| Gauss / QFF nodes (`references/qff.md`) | Not a discrepancy figure at all | Not a net |

For a noisy plant GP, the difference between a good Joe-Kuo Sobol' and a good CBC lattice, after `F_Λ⁻¹`, is usually smaller than kernel misspecification (`references/gp-kernels.md`). The figure of merit matters when explaining *why* a `z` or a set of direction numbers is good — not as a substitute for actually looking at qTS path diversity and `ℓ` bound-hits.

## What not to mix

- Reporting a Walsh `P_α` for a rank-1 `z` produced by `scripts/cbc_fft.py`.
- Reporting a Fourier `P2` for Sobol' points and calling that "CBC."
- Using the same generating vector as both a lattice `z` and a digital matrix — they live in different algebras.
- Comparing raw `P2` and `P_α^Wal` values as numbers — different kernels, different duals, different scales, not comparable at all.

If a Walsh CBC is ever needed in code, that's a new script entirely: generating matrices or polynomial-lattice coefficients, a base-2 Walsh kernel, an FFT over `𝔽2^m`. It does not extend `scripts/cbc_fft.py` with a flag — the two live in genuinely different number systems.
