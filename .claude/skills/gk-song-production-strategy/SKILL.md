# GK Song Production Strategy

## HOOKS AND BRIDGES NEED REAL PUNCH — a rallying-cry moment, not a flat recitation (Joe, 29 Oct 2026)

Joe's direct correction: the hook isn't landing with a strong enough
punch, and the bridge isn't hitting hard enough either — he wants
something people would actually shout back, a "my kingdom"-level
declaration, a line built for a crowd to sing/chant along to live,
not a competently-written line that just sits there. This applies on
top of (not instead of) the existing structural rules — every hook
and every bridge, regardless of today's content category or vibe,
needs this specific quality of impact:
- **The hook's signature line must be a declaration, not a
  description.** Not "this is about courage" — a line that CLAIMS
  something ("this is MY kingdom," "this ground is MINE," "WE are the
  guul") in the first person or direct address, short enough and
  rhythmically simple enough that a crowd could shout it back after
  one listen. Favor a short declarative claim over a longer poetic
  image at the hook's peak moment.
- **The bridge is a pushing moment, not just a quiet one.** The
  existing rule (beat drops to just the Kaban, spoken-word, reflective)
  is still right for TONE, but the words themselves still need to push
  — a rallying address to the listener/crowd, a call-and-answer beat,
  or a line that sets up the final hook's bigger swell rather than
  just musing quietly. "Reflective in sound" and "flat in impact" are
  not the same thing — keep the hushed production, raise the lyrical
  stakes.
- **Test every hook/bridge draft against this question before
  finalizing:** would a crowd actually shout this back, or is it just
  a nice line to read? If it only reads well, rewrite the peak line as
  a direct, short, first-person or direct-address claim before moving
  on — this check applies on every song, every day, regardless of
  content category (see rotation rule below) or vibe.

## CONTENT-CATEGORY ROTATION is mandatory — vibe/form rotation alone is not enough (Joe, 29 Oct 2026)

Joe's direct correction, after several days straight of Kaban Rap Drop
songs (Kaban-maker, camel-herder, goldsmith, letter-to-mother,
athlete-in-training): "same strategy... somebody talking life
difficult, okay, or the survivor... you should be something love,
something romantic, something patriotic, something like enjoying
song... no matter how many times I teach you, you will not change."
Rotating the VIBE (cinematic/triumphant/reflective/club-pulse) and the
POETIC FORM while the underlying CONTENT CATEGORY stays "hardship /
perseverance / everyday-struggle" every single day is the exact
failure this flags — different packaging, same emotional note. Vibe
and form rotation were never a substitute for this; they sit on top
of it.

**Fix, binding on the daily routine from now on, STEP 1 of every song
(before vibe/form/language):** first pick the CONTENT CATEGORY itself,
and rotate it so the same category never runs 2 days straight and
"everyday-trade/hardship/legacy" (the lane this skill leaned on almost
exclusively) is just ONE option among several, never the default.
Categories to rotate across:
- **Love/romance** — Qaraami Love Duet mode (section 8) is the
  reference; courtship, longing, devotion, a parting vow. Not a
  hardship story with a love line added.
- **Celebration/fun/joy** — a wedding, a dance, a community
  celebration, pure good news; Dhaanto/Buraanbur energy; nothing heavy
  at all, no struggle framing.
- **Patriotic/pride** — flag, homeland, unity, GK-identity; the
  catalog-proven patriotic-anthem lane (section 11) — pride and
  legacy, not a survival story wearing a flag.
- **Playful/lighthearted** — humor, banter, a cheeky/fun narrator
  voice; genuinely light, not profound.
- **Everyday-trade/legacy/hardship** — what this skill defaulted to;
  keep it, but only as one rotating option, never picked two days in a
  row and never the fallback when nothing else comes to mind.

Check the last 3-4 days' actual CONTENT CATEGORY (not just their vibe
label) before picking today's, the same anti-repetition discipline
STEP 1/STEP 4 already apply to vibe and form. A love song can still be
triumphant; a patriotic song can still be reflective — category and
vibe are independent axes, rotate both.

**Concrete theme words Joe gave directly (29 Oct 2026) to pull from
inside the categories above — don't let these sit unused, actually
write toward them in rotation, and give each its own "push" per the
hook/bridge rule above:**
- Liberty, freedom, sovereignty, dignity, respect — patriotic/pride
  category, dignity-forward rather than only flag/victory imagery.
- Unsatisfied love, an unbroken/unbreakable heart — love/romance
  category, the ache/longing register, not only the happy-courtship
  register Qaraami Duet defaults to.
  **Reference title bank Joe gave directly (29 Oct 2026) for this
  category — real titles, usable as-is or as the direct pattern for a
  new title, not just inspiration to half-copy:** Naftayda Ku Kuursay
  (The One Wrapped Around My Soul), Boqorada Jaceylka (The Queen of
  Love), Dhiigga Wadnahayga (The Blood of My Heart), Xiddigtii
  Noloshayda (The Star of My Life), Caashaq Aan Dhamaanayn (An
  Endless Love). Draw a love/romance day's title from this bank (or
  a title in the same direct, devotional-declaration pattern) before
  inventing a new one from scratch.
- Forever life, fantasy — a mythic/aspirational register (legacy that
  outlives the singer, a larger-than-life imagined future) — fits the
  personal-legacy/GK-identity angle or Visionary Empire mode.
- Irritation, irritable — real frustration/conflict as its own honest
  emotional note, not softened into generic "struggle" — a song can
  be ABOUT being fed up, not just about enduring.
- On the hood, on the block, surviving the street, serving life — the
  Street Reality/Wake-Up Call mode (section 16) already built for
  exactly this; use it, don't let it sit unused while defaulting to
  the gentler everyday-trade lane.
- Helping — service/community care as its own theme (someone who
  shows up for others), distinct from the everyday-trade-hardship
  lane even when the subject also has a trade.
- Jealousy, selfish, greedy, unhelper — the shadow/vice side, usable
  as a warning or a contrast figure in a Street Reality-style song
  (what NOT to become), not something GK glorifies as the narrator's
  own voice.
- Self-official, self-independent, hard survivor, hustler, money
  maker, intelligent, individual, success, individual success — an
  ambition/self-made-success register: a narrator who built himself
  up alone, smart and self-reliant, measuring his own win — distinct
  from the Visionary Empire mode's stadium-scale ambition; this one
  is personal and individual-scale, one person's hustle and come-up.

## English-language sections must follow standard grammar (Joe, 29 Oct 2026)

Apply these as a hard checklist to every English lyric line (and every
English line in a bilingual chorus/hook) before finalizing a song doc:
subject-verb-object order; consistent tense within a sentence; correct
articles (a/an for new/general, "the" for something already
established); subject-verb agreement (she works, they work); never
drop the subject ("it is raining," not "is raining"); every line a
complete thought with a main clause; keep lines short and punchy
rather than one long run-on; correct prepositions (interested in,
good at, afraid of); prefer active voice; correct question word order
when a line is phrased as a question; keep a modifier next to the
thing it describes; use plain linking words (and, but, because, so)
to connect ideas; capitalize sentence starts and proper names; normal
end punctuation. This doesn't flatten the song's style — short,
declarative, grammatically clean lines are also exactly what the
hook/bridge punch rule above is asking for.

## Real Somali trap/drill opener vocabulary + UK drill texture option (Joe, 29 Oct 2026)

Joe gave a categorized list of real Somali words with their real
meanings, specifically for trap/drill-leaning songs — use these
(never invented substitutes) when a hook, intro, or verse opener needs
this kind of weight. Same "real words only" iron rule as
`gk-gabay-blueprint`'s vocabulary table; this list extends that table
for the harder/street-leaning registers:

- **Aggressive drill/trap openers:** Kici (wake up/rise/ignite — instant
  hype before the beat drops), Walaalo (brothers — shouting out the
  crew/day-ones), Ordan (running/chasing — fast-paced drill motion),
  Gariir (tremble/shake — tells the listener the bass is about to hit).
- **Hard street storytelling:** Xabsi (prison/cage — a heavy, real word
  common in UK-Somali drill), Iska jir (watch out/beware — street
  awareness, fake friends), Kalinimada (loneliness/isolation — a deep
  introspective intro), Madow (dark/black — a moody late-night vibe).
- **Deep cultural metaphors:** Gabay (poem — links modern bars to
  centuries of Somali poetic history, same form already in this
  skill's rotation), Ciidda (the soil/the land — roots meeting where
  you stand now), Dhiig (blood — loyalty, passion, or pain), Masiibo
  (disaster/tragedy — signals the lyrics are about to get dangerous).
- **Short cinematic starters:** Halkan (right here — establishes
  ground/presence immediately), Erayada (the words — puts full focus
  on the lyricism), Bari (east — a proud nod to East Africa and
  nomadic heritage).

**UK drum/British drill texture:** on a Street Reality/drill-leaning
song, it's fine to lean into UK drill-style drum programming (per
section 13's 142-143 BPM UK drill reference) as a deliberate,
occasional production choice — not every song, but a real option in
the rotation when the content category calls for that harder street
register.

## Core theme is appreciation/blessing + "Jannah beneath her feet" — not a life-event timeline (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Joe rejected a stanza listing "when I grew up, when I fell, when I got
married" as generic and meaningless — a timeline of events is not the
same as honoring her. The real theme, per Joe and matching what worked
in ChatGPT's version: **you can never repay or match what she did —
you are not on her level — so the song's job is appreciation and
blessing, not narrating a life story.** The specific, central image to
use (a known hadith, real and widely cited — not invented): **Jannah
(Paradise) lies beneath the mother's feet.** This should be a core
recurring theme throughout the song, not a line tacked onto a closing
prayer — e.g. a stanza stating plainly "what you gave me I cannot
repay you, I am not on your level, Paradise is beneath your feet, and
that is the truth," woven in alongside the du'a/prayer block (see
below), not separate from it. Avoid generic "stages of life" stanzas
(grew up / fell / married / etc.) entirely for this song type — they
read as filler, not tribute.

## A mother-tribute song is not a love song — ground it in literal maternal acts, not reciprocal/romantic phrasing (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Two concrete content errors Joe caught in the same Hooyo rewrite,
apply both as standing rules for any Hooyo/mother-tribute song:
- **Never write a line that trades on reciprocity/romantic logic**
  ("do you remember me the way I remember you") — that is love-song
  structure, not mother-song structure. A mother's love in this genre
  is described as unconditional and one-directional from before you
  could even reciprocate: she remembers you from the day you were
  born, from when you were in her womb — not "the way you remember
  her." Ground every "does she remember/love me" idea in that framing,
  never a symmetrical exchange.
- **Never use a vague developmental-stage word like "youth" for
  infancy.** When the song is about being a baby/infant, say so
  literally and specifically: her carrying you (womb/pregnancy), her
  staying awake through the night while the father was out at work,
  her nursing you, her being the one who physically cared for an
  infant who could do nothing for itself. "Youth" reads as
  teenage/growing-up, which is a completely different life stage from
  what Joe means by "when I was nothing" — always ask which specific
  age/stage before picking a word like that.

## Never recycle the same lyric line across rewrites — write genuinely fresh content every time (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Across the Hooyo rewrites, the opening line "Saddex sano ayaan ku arag waayay"
(three years I haven't seen you) survived unchanged through several
rewrites while only the production notes/structure changed around it.
Joe's correction: changing the arrangement, BPM, or adding new sections
is NOT the same as writing a new song — if a rewrite keeps the same
core lyric lines Joe already said weren't landing, from his side it
looks like nothing changed, however different the surrounding notes
are. Every real rewrite needs genuinely new lyric content, especially
the opening line, not the same line carried forward under new staging.
If a specific line/image was never actually flagged as the problem,
confirm with Joe before assuming it's safe to keep reusing it.

## A real du'a/prayer block + communal backing vocals, confirmed by two outside AI tools (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Joe ran the identical Hooyo brief through ChatGPT and DeepSeek side by
side and judged both better than our version at that point. Comparing
both outputs (never storing their lyric text — pattern only, same
no-reproduction rule as any reference track) surfaced two techniques
both tools converged on independently, which is a strong signal they're
real, expected features of this song type, not a style gimmick:

- **Give the du'a/prayer a real, dedicated block — don't just texture
  the song with faith.** Both tools closed the song on an explicit
  prayer for the mother (long life, health, Jannatul Firdaws), given
  its own section near the end, carrying real emotional weight — not
  a passing reference woven into a verse. For Hooyo-tribute songs,
  include a clearly-labeled prayer/du'a section as a structural beat,
  the way real Somali mother-tribute songs often do.
- **Backing/communal vocals ("aah" harmonies, call-and-response echo
  of a line like the mother's name) in the chorus**, not a single lead
  voice throughout. This matches real buraanbur/communal-song tradition
  and makes a personal tribute read as "everyone's mother," not just
  one person's. Note this in the Suno style tag as a backing-vocal /
  choir layer under the chorus specifically.
- **What NOT to copy from either tool**: DeepSeek's version used a slow
  60-70 BPM, "almost whispered" delivery throughout — reject this. It's
  exactly the soft/thin delivery already corrected against (see below,
  "don't go too soft"). The medium-energy, 92 BPM, full-voiced call
  stands; a real du'a block and backing harmonies both work fine at
  medium energy — they don't require going soft.
- A short, clearly-labeled spoken-word moment *as one bounded section*
  (not the whole song, and not used in place of actually singing) is
  fine — consistent with the already-corrected Suno tool lesson below.
  Both outside tools used a brief spoken/intro moment this way.

## Open on the universal ache, not the gentle image — and don't go too soft (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Final content lesson from the "Hooyo" rewrites: even with the right
structure, Somali-sung, and real imagery, a draft that opened on a
gentle image (her hands protecting you as a baby) still read as too
soft to grab a listener before they scroll past. Joe's fix, apply
generally to any emotionally personal song, not just Hooyo tracks:
- **Open on the rawest, most universal version of the ache, not a tender
  scene-setting image.** For a mother-tribute song specifically: lead
  with direct separation/longing (years since you've seen her, starting
  to forget her face) — the exact feeling real people expressed in the
  comments Joe showed, not a soft "when you were a baby" opener. Gentle
  stage-of-life imagery still belongs in the song — just later, once the
  hook has already hit.
- **"Reflective" and "intimate" do not mean thin or whisper-soft.** A
  personal song still needs medium energy and real instrumental presence
  (drum/Kaban audible under every stanza, not isolated to a bridge or
  refrain) — Joe's words: "the sound has to be Somali, not soft... plus
  energy." A hushed, delicate arrangement risks losing the listener
  before the emotional payoff lands, however good the writing is.
- **The retention principle from the daily routine's own rules applies
  here too, explicitly stated as feedback this time:** if the first few
  seconds don't hit, the listener skips before the good part at the end
  ever plays — so the hardest-hitting line goes first, every time, on
  every song, not just the high-energy modes.

## Suno rendered as spoken word, not sung — CORRECTED root cause (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Joe generated a song doc on Suno and it came out as flat spoken English
narration with no melody. **First diagnosis was wrong and has been
corrected:** it was NOT the "close your eyes" rhetorical-address line
itself, and that device is fine and wanted — Joe explicitly asked for it
back in a later revision, written as the singer's own sung line direct
to the listener ("close your eyes, think of your mother, how could I
live without you") rather than a narrator's aside. **The real cause,
confirmed from Joe's screenshots:** he had pasted the song doc into
Suno's **Speech** tool (text-to-speech/narration) instead of the
**Songs** tool (music generation) — the "SPEECH" label on every dead
result and the Script/Tone fields in his screenshot confirmed this.
Always check which Suno tool is in use before diagnosing a flat result
as a lyric-writing problem — ask Joe to confirm he's on the Songs tab,
Lyrics box for the lyrics, Style box for the style tag, before assuming
the song doc itself needs a content rewrite.

The line-by-line Somali/English alternation WAS still a real, separate
issue worth avoiding (translation-reading risk independent of which
tool is used) — that part of the fix below stays:
- **Lyrics meant to be sung go in ONE language per line, Somali-primary
  unless the mode calls for English-led.** If Joe needs the English
  meaning for his own reference, put it in parentheses directly under
  the Somali line and label the whole lyric block "NOT to be sung,
  translation note only" — never alternate sung-Somali/sung-English line
  by line, that reads as a cue to talk-through a translation.
- **Never write a line that instructs the listener/vocalist to do
  something** (e.g. "close your eyes," "picture her face") as if it's
  stage narration — that gets sung or spoken literally and breaks the
  song's voice. Direct address and raw imagery (section above) still
  apply — just keep it as something the singer says AS the song, not an
  aside to the listener about how to listen.
- **State explicitly in the Suno style tag that the vocal is fully SUNG
  with a real melody, not spoken word or narration** — don't assume this
  is obvious; say it outright, especially for intimate/reflective modes
  where a hushed delivery could otherwise read as spoken-word to the
  model.
- Keep bracketed section directions ([Stanza 1], [Bridge], [Outro])
  production-only — BPM/instrument cues, not phrased as something a
  vocalist would read aloud.

## Every topic must carry real, researched meaning — not just a word as a title (Joe, 21 Oct 2026)

Joe's correction after "Hooyada Xaafadda" (21 Oct Kaban Rap Drop): the song
used "Hooyo" (mother) as a loose title/motif for a nurse-caregiver story,
but never actually grounded itself in what "Hooyo" is supposed to carry —
real weight, real meaning. Joe's words: "every subject has to have the
things you talking... every song has to have meaning, or the society."
He should never have to re-explain this per song — it's a standing
discipline from now on:

- **Don't pick a concept and float free of its real substance.** If the
  concept touches motherhood, draw on the real, known reference points
  that actually carry that weight: the Islamic teachings honoring
  mothers (e.g. the well-known hadith placing Paradise at the feet of
  mothers, the Qur'anic emphasis on a mother's sacrifice and the duty of
  kindness to parents — check and cite real sources, same fact-discipline
  as everywhere else in this skill, never invent a "teaching"), the
  universal child's-duty themes (gratitude, care in old age, honoring her
  sacrifice), and/or a specific international angle if the brief is going
  global rather than homeland-specific. The concept has to connect
  concretely to the subject's real cultural, religious, or societal
  meaning — not just use the word as a flavor/title.
- **This generalizes beyond "Hooyo."** Any theme this skill reaches for
  (a trade, a historical moment, a social-conscience topic, a patriotic
  anthem) needs the same discipline: know what the subject actually
  means to people — the real tradition, teaching, value, or social weight
  behind it — and build the song around that substance, not around a
  catchy word wearing the subject's name. A title alone is not a concept.
- **Do this automatically, without Joe having to spell it out per song.**
  The research/grounding step (section 1's concept pick, this skill's
  fact-check discipline, and `gk-gabay-blueprint`'s checked-facts
  practice) already exists for exactly this reason — apply it as a
  default checkpoint before finalizing any theme, every time, not only
  when Joe flags a miss.

**Critical follow-up correction, same day — citing a fact is NOT the same
as making someone feel it (Joe, 21 Oct 2026):** the first fix above was
still wrong. Adding real hadith citations to "Hooyada Xaafadda" made it
more scholarly, not more felt — Joe's exact words: "you didn't change
anything... you cannot compare anything... it has to feel the mother."
A citation is a support, never the device itself. What actually works,
and what Joe asked for directly:
- **Concrete, second-person, stage-of-life imagery** — not a concept
  talked about, a person talked TO. For a mother: she protected you as
  an infant, she looked after you growing up, she was still there after
  you married and built your own life — specific physical images (a
  wound cleaned before you finished crying, worry and prayer continuing
  long after you left home), not abstractions like "sacrifice" or
  "honor" sitting alone.
- **The incomparability has to be stated concretely, not asserted.**
  Joe's own example: you can travel to Belgium, to Saudi Arabia, ten
  times over — nobody else on any of those trips ever wakes up at
  midnight for you the way she does. That specific, almost mundane
  image (a real person checking on you at 3am) hits harder than any
  abstract claim that "no one compares."
  Use this same technique for other subjects too: don't just assert a
  thing is special/unmatched — show one small, specific, true-to-life
  moment that proves it.
- **The listener must feel the subject, not learn about it — "doesn't
  matter if you are a woman or a man."** Write in direct second-person
  address ("you," not "she is honored by her community") whenever the
  subject is a person someone has a real relationship with (mother,
  father, a lost loved one, home itself) — this makes the song land on
  anyone listening, not just a Somali/homeland audience.

**Confirmed real reference pattern — Somali Hooyo-tribute songs (Joe, 21
Oct 2026, "Suldaan Seeraar — Hooyo Ha Iilan Hooyoy," DJ Rockets Lyrics
video):** Joe showed screenshots of this real, popular track's on-screen
lyrics specifically to show the correct technique — pattern captured
here, no lyric text stored or reproduced (same rule as every other
reference in this skill):
- **Structure:** short four-line stanzas, each one stacking a vivid
  comparison/image for the mother (treasure, world, guide, the ground
  everything else stands on), and every stanza closes on the SAME short
  refrain line. The refrain isn't a separate hook section bolted onto a
  verse/chorus skeleton — it IS the structure, repeating after every
  stanza and carrying the emotional anchor by sheer repetition.
- **Faith woven in as lived detail, not cited as a lesson:** her prayers,
  her devotion, her faith appear as images of who she is and what she
  does, not as an explained teaching or a quoted source. This is the
  opposite move from this skill's earlier (wrong) attempt to fix a flat
  draft by adding hadith citations — here the faith content is simply
  part of describing her, same register as describing her hands or her
  home.
- **Biological/life-stage truth as real imagery:** carrying the child,
  nursing the child — stated plainly as fact, not softened into
  abstraction. Confirms the "concrete stage-of-life imagery" lesson
  above is exactly right, and shows the specific register to pitch it
  in (plain, direct, unembellished fact, not poetic distance).
- **When Joe asks for a "Hooyo song" or references this track/style by
  name, use this exact structural device** (quatrain + repeated closing
  refrain line, comparison-stacking praise, faith and biology as lived
  detail) rather than the usual verse/hook/bridge shape used elsewhere
  in this skill — it's a distinct, confirmed-good structure for this
  specific subject, the same way the Qaraami Love Duet (section 8) is a
  confirmed template for its own subject.
- **When Joe says a draft "doesn't make sense" or "you didn't change
  anything," that means the FEELING is missing, not that a fact was
  wrong.** Don't respond by adding more research or more citations —
  respond by making the imagery more specific and more direct-address.
  These are different failure modes and need different fixes.

Why this exists: rotating the poetic-form *label* each day (Gabay one day,
Jiifto the next) was not enough — every song still had the same skeleton
(spoken Kaban intro → stat-heavy verse → "word! word!" call-response hook →

## 1. Concept & Emotional Core — decide this FIRST, before language or beat

Before anything else, answer:
- **The story:** historical/regional roots (e.g. Southwest — Caro Bay iyo
  Bakool), a modern street anthem, an everyday trade elevated into legend

A topic chosen first and a mood bolted on after is exactly the failure mode

## 2. Linguistic & Lyrical Architecture

- **Language:** a seamless blend of **Af-Maay**, standard **Af-Soomaali**,
  and rhythmic **English** hooks. Af-Maay + Southwest roots is a real,
  standing option now — not a one-time special. In the daily routine's

- **The hook/chorus:** must be a true anthem — memorable, "the beating heart

  call-and-response shape every time — vary the hook's shape day to day
  (a single repeated line, a question-and-answer with the chant, a hummable
  short phrase) the same way the structure itself should vary.
- **Rhyme scheme & cadence:** engineered to ride over heavy bass while
  keeping the traditional poetic meter intact underneath.

## 3. Cadence & Meter (Miisaanka Gabayga) — classical discipline, not vibes

Three disciplines, always applied together:

1. **The Ruling Letter (Xaraf Raac):** pick ONE consonant (or treat all
   vowels as one sound) for the stanza. Every key line anchors a word to
   that sound — e.g. ruling letter "B": Bir (iron), Biyo (water), Baahin
   (need), Bilan (crescent/beginning). This is the rhythmic drumbeat before
   any instrument plays. Note the ruling letter at the top of the song doc,
   same as a Gabay day already does.
2. **Internal Assonance & Vowels (Dhawaaq iyo Jiib):** beyond the first
   letter, match internal vowel harmony and syllable length line to line —
   the "wave crashing in a precise, repeating rhythm" feel of a chanted
   buraanbur.
3. **The Breath and Cadence (Neefta Gabayga):** control the pause between
   lines deliberately — the silence carries as much weight as an 808 drop.
   Mark intentional pauses in the lyric doc (e.g. an em dash or a line break)
   so the delivery note is explicit, not implied.

## 4. The Thematic Focus (The Message)

Use the gabay/buraanbur form to tell stories of **resilience, craftsmanship,
unity, and heritage** — elevate everyday trades and ordinary moments into
timeless legends, the same weight classical gabay gave to real historical
figures. Keep the language sharp, poetic, and emotionally charged, using
authentic Af-Soomaali and Af-Maay idioms. This is the fix for topics that
read like a researched news brief: a concrete subject is still required
(per the daily routine's STEP 2), but it must be told as a *story about a
person or craft*, not a summary of a headline.

## 5. Musical Instrumentation & Frequency (The Soundbed)

- **"The Drop" (Joe, 19 Oct 2026):** on any high-energy song (club/drill/

  build] ... [DROP → CHORUS — bass hits hard]") so it's an intentional

- **Percussion:** heavy Dhaanto and traditional drums layered with crisp

  **Explicitly NO emotional/melodic piano lead** — that reads soft, not

  nighttime-city atmosphere, cinematic tension, "cold and dangerous," raw

  the energy lane for when Joe asks for something "with no energy" fixed,

  Studio brand and setting the nighttime-city scene in a few fragments →

## 16. Street Reality / Wake-Up Call mode (Joe, 20 Oct 2026)

Joe's follow-up to Visionary Empire — same strength and pump, but aimed at
youth with a real social-conscience message instead of pure triumph.

- **Mood/theme territory:** a wake-up call, not a celebration — the hard
  truths of street life for real: drug struggle, unemployment, a grown man

  Not glorifying any of it — the point is tough-love education, waking the

  the Visionary Empire mode's "escape the trap, build the empire instead"
  arc — this mode is the warning, that mode is the way out).
  **Never glorify drug use/dealing or crime — depict the cost, not the
  appeal**, same boundary as everywhere else in this skill set.
- **Delivery/regional flavor:** still strong, pumping energy — Joe named

  flow references (scene/genre anchors only, never imitating a specific
  named artist's voice — same rule as every reference artist in this
  skill). Reads as grounded, real, street-level storytelling delivery
  rather than the stadium-chant bravado of Visionary Empire.
- **Production:** Dhaanto rhythm woven into the beat underneath the
  modern trap/hip-hop production (Joe specifically asked for Dhaanto
  here) — keep the Somali rhythmic identity present even in a US-flavored
  street-rap mode, same house-sound-signature discipline as every other
  mode.
- **Structure:** works well as a verse-driven, storytelling structure
  (less hook-repetition-heavy than Visionary Empire) — each verse can
  carry a different piece of the reality check (money/unemployment, the

  turn where the song calls the listener to wake up and rise, rather than
  ending in despair.
- **Must hit as hard as Visionary Empire from the first line (Joe, 20 Oct
  2026 — "Toos Xaafadda" first draft read too slow/flat compared to

## Kaban must pop — a standing production rule (Joe, 20 Oct 2026)

The Kaban can't just sit as background texture — every song needs at
least one deliberate moment where it hits strongly and is unmistakably
the feature, not just color underneath the beat. This is on top of (not
a replacement for) the existing house-sound rules: the closing "audio
logo" pluck (brand guide, CLAUDE.md) and a Kaban line entering on the
hook/outro in modes like Visionary Empire and Street Reality. When
writing a Suno style tag, make sure at least one specific moment is
called out by name for the Kaban to really pop — a solo break, a
beat-drop where everything else cuts out except the Kaban, or a hard
accented phrase riding right on top of the hook — not just "Kaban
present throughout."

**Pentatonic scale (Joe, 20 Oct 2026):** the Kaban's melodic lines/solo
moments should be written on a pentatonic scale — name it explicitly in
the Suno style tag ("Kaban solo in a pentatonic scale/run") wherever a
featured Kaban moment is called for, alongside the existing traditional-
tuning language already used elsewhere in this skill.
